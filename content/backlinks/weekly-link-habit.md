> [!TLDR]
> - Pick 2 or 3 categories of sites that fit your business, and shortlist 20 prospects.
> - Build one reusable submission kit so each submission takes minutes.
> - Submit five a week on a fixed Monday-to-Friday rhythm.
> - Approval is not a link. Open the live page and check, then keep only what sent visitors or relationships.

## Pick two or three categories that fit

**Start narrow: two or three kinds of site, chosen for where your buyers already look.** The categories differ by business:

- SaaS: software directories, startup launches, developer resources.
- Local tutoring company: local listings, educational resources, community partnerships.
- Affiliate site: publishing original resources, relevant discussion communities, editorial outreach.

From those categories, shortlist 20 prospects and score each one as in *Know what a link is worth*.

## Build one submission kit

Most submissions ask for the same things. Gather them once in a folder: website URL, short description, long description, logo, screenshots, founder bio and story, tagline, project examples.

**Keep the name, the one-line description and the logo identical everywhere**, so Google and AI tools recognise every listing as the same product. Tailor the longer descriptions to each audience instead of pasting the same promotional copy everywhere.

Pick 20 to 30 launch platforms and software directories where your buyers look, not 300.

> [!TRICK] The five-a-week routine
> - **Monday:** research and qualify five prospects.
> - **Tuesday:** prepare materials from your kit.
> - **Wednesday:** submit or pitch.
> - **Thursday:** follow up where it makes sense.
> - **Friday:** check approvals and update your tracker.
>
> Where a platform needs original content or participation instead of a form, spend that slot on the contribution.

## Track the link, not the approval

A site accepting your submission does not mean you have a backlink. Visit the published page and check the actual link: it may be missing, unclickable or marked nofollow.

> [!TRICK] Tracker columns
> | Field | What to record |
> | --- | --- |
> | Website | Platform or publication |
> | Category | Directory, community, PR, etc. |
> | Target URL | Your page you want people to find |
> | Submission date | When you applied |
> | Status | Researching, submitted, approved, rejected |
> | Published URL | The exact live page |
> | Link status | Clickable, missing, restricted |
> | Link attributes | Followed, nofollow, sponsored, UGC |
> | Referral traffic | Visits from analytics |
> | Notes | Requirements, pricing, next action |

After a month, ask four questions: which sites approved you, which sent visitors, which put you in front of the right audience, and which led to relationships or more mentions. Put next month's time into those.

## Find prospects in the right lists

These lists are for discovery: pick only entries where you qualify and your buyers would look. Launch platforms and startup directories are in *Find your first 100 users*.

> [!TRICK] Where to look, by category
> - **Review profiles:** complete your G2 and Capterra profiles, ask customers for real reviews, and give reviewers access and current facts.
> - **Developer and open source** (needs a real, working project with docs): GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg, GitHub Pages, PyPI, npm, crates.io, RubyGems, Packagist, Docker Hub, F-Droid, Open VSX, VS Code Marketplace, Firefox Add-ons, WordPress.org plugins, Drupal Projects, Hugging Face, StackBlitz, CodePen, Replit.
> - **Industry-specific** (only if you qualify): Tripadvisor, Hipcamp, The Dyrt, HappyCow, Houzz, Thumbtack, Justia, Avvo, Martindale-Hubbell, FindLaw, Healthgrades, WebMD Care, Vitals, MERLOT, OER Commons, Share My Lesson, GreatNonprofits, Candid/GuideStar, Charity Navigator, VolunteerMatch.
> - **Creator profiles and storefronts** (only ones you'd use anyway): Linktree, Beacons, Bio.site, Lnk.Bio, Campsite, Milkshake, Taplink, AllMyLinks, HeyLink, Bio.fm, Portaly, Carrd, Ko-fi, Buy Me a Coffee, Payhip, Gumroad, itch.io, Bandcamp, Flickr, 500px.
> - **Government, supplier and business listings:** claim the ones you're eligible for and keep your details identical across all of them.
> - **More:** a searchable list at https://backlinkforme.com/backlinks (the list author's own site).

> [!RISK] Empty profiles and reciprocal badges
> Listings that require a link back to them, and mass submissions to many directories, can count as link schemes under Google's spam policies. A profile created only to hold your URL adds nothing. Fill in every profile you create, and skip directories whose free route demands a reciprocal badge.

> [!DO] Build your submission kit
> Create a folder named "Link kit" with your URL, a one-line and a one-paragraph description, your logo, three screenshots and a two-sentence founder bio. Next Monday, use it on your first five prospects.

> [!CHECK] What does each weekday of the five-a-week backlink routine cover?
> Monday research and qualify five prospects, Tuesday prepare materials, Wednesday submit or pitch, Thursday follow up, Friday check approvals and update your tracker.

> [!CHECK] A directory emails to say your listing is approved. Do you have the backlink?
> Not yet. Open the published page and check the link itself, because it may be missing, unclickable or nofollow.

> [!CHECK] Why build a submission kit before you start asking for backlinks?
> Most submissions ask for the same things, so gathering them once makes each submission take minutes.
