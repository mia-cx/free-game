### HOW X DECIDES WHO SEES YOUR LAUNCH

X has a problem.

350 million posts go up every day on X but each person scrolling can only see a few hundred.

So X has to find the posts you'd like most, then rank them.

Think of a recruiter with a million applicants.

She can't interview everyone, so she makes a shortlist. Then she ranks the shortlist.

She finds past candidates with potential and looks for new candidates who has experience in the field.

X follows the same analogy

X pulls candidates from three places:

> Thunder finds recent posts from accounts you follow.

> Phoenix retrieval turns your history into a picture of your interests, then finds posts that match it.

> SimClusters groups accounts by who engages with what, then finds posts your kind of people like.

Round 1: get sourced

The first posts are pulled from your follower/following circle

That's why a founder with 100k followers is more likely to get 5M views on his launch.

Sourcing is also the round you control most, because you decide who sees the post first

Round 2: get ranked

Phoenix predicts what each viewer will do with your post, then adds it up. These are the current weights

But don’t obsess over these numbers

X says: weights multiply the odds that one viewer takes an action, and rare actions get bigger weights.

Here's what it means for your launch:

The main post has to stand alone.

You cannot distribute attention across 10 different posts

X has to keep one post per conversation in the feed.

So if you spread your launch across 5 tweets, 4 of them will flop and your non followers will never get them recommended.

X is building one feed for one person.

You need to win that person, then repeat it a million times.


### THE "WHY DO I CARE" FILTER

I will explain something below but the BEST way to actually understand what i’m saying is to get on any subway and watch people scroll.

Get in a subway and observe people on their phone

Try to figure why they decide to stop scrolling on a post and watch it

What gets them to care?

And they decide in under a second. That's the beauty of it

Everything on X has to get through that filter.

We call it the "why do I care" filter

And this is perhaps THE MOST important filter for any launch

On X you're fighting for a stranger’s attention, someone who owes you nothing.

Our mind turns off and immediately defaults to scrolling when it sees some words/phrases

These are things you should NOT do:

- "We're excited to announce"

- A logo animation

- A niche category launch

- Twelve seconds of a founder talking before anything happens

Want to get someone’s attention, do this instead:

- A $20M raise(because the number says this matters)

- A fight someone already has an opinion on (topics like datacenters, OpenAI)

- A result so big it sounds fake.

Now look at what people comment on launches.

Almost nobody asks about your features.

They say "congrats, that's huge" about the raise, or they argue about what your launch means.

So before you write a word of copy, you need to know what people will argue about.

Rule: the viewer owes you nothing. Give them a reason to care in the first line, or they never meet your product.

### BEFORE LAUNCH, STEP 2: PASS THE HUMAN TEST

A narrative gets someone to stop.

The demo decides whether they believe you.

Every launch has to make the viewer think "oh shit, this is real"?

How do you make this happen?

Push the product to its extreme, and make the proof real.

Look at the latest Tesla Roadster launch for example

It launches off the ramp, flips in the air and lands on its wheels.

Then it jumps over a whole row of Teslas

That's MrBeast logic.

Jumping one car is interesting. Jumping the entire fleet is something you share with 10 friends

And they did it live, in front of a crowd, on a livestream.

If they hadn't, you'd assume it was CGI.

It still broke people's brains.

You don't need rockets to do this. You need to answer two questions.

1. What's the most extreme thing your product can do?

2. How do you make people believe it happened?.

Then show those exact moments in the video.


### BEFORE LAUNCH, STEP 3: FOLLOWERS VS CONNECTIONS

A follower is someone who can see you.

A connection is someone with an audience who will answer your text.

Your investor. Your first customer. Your old boss.

Remember round 1. Your followers feed Thunder for your account, and that's where it stops.

When a connection quotes tweets your launch, it's a new post from them, and it goes to their followers too. 

Before the launch, you should ask all your connections to quote tweet it.

The biggest realization I've had about this came from JD Ross, the co-founder of Opendoor.

We ran his launch for WithCoverage, and it booked 600 demos.

[embedded post 2087565125137912223]
Afterward he told me something I think about constantly.

He could have asked someone powerful for an intro. That costs a favor and gets you one meeting.

Asking that same person to support his launch costs the exact same favor, and he got 1000x the leverage.

There is no higher-leverage ask you can make of the people you know.

1. Export everything. Pull the followers of anyone on your team with an account.

2. Add the people no export shows. Investors, advisors, customers, beta testers, portfolio founders.

3. Rank by reach into your customer

4. Tier it. Your top 25 or so get a message from the founder personally,

Bad message: "Hey, we're launching Tuesday, would love a retweet!"

Good message: "Hey, here's what we've been working on. Would you quote this? Here's an example of a great one from an investor on a similar launch."
