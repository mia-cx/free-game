There’s a method taught at Y Combinator that turns 4 weeks of GTM research into 3 hrs.

And no, it’s not typing “research the market for X” into Claude.

You start by feeding it raw material:
→ 25-40 competitor websites, fully scraped (pricing pages, case studies, docs)
→ The last few earnings calls from the 3-5 biggest incumbents
→ 100+ customer reviews (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, app stores)
→ Every Reddit or forum thread where people vent about the category

Then ask questions that force it to think, not summarize:

1. “What do the winners in this market know that their customers would never admit?”
2. “List the beliefs this whole industry takes for granted. For each one, what would need to happen for it to be false?”

In about 20 min, you’ll see where everyone agrees, where everyone is blind, and which customer pains nobody is building for.

Now flip it. Pick your best angle and tell Claude to tear it apart:

“Steelman this thesis like a top-tier VC would. Then show me exactly where it fails.”

Do a few rounds of that. 3 hours later, you’ll have a first GTM hypothesis that sounds like it came from a 10-year industry veteran.

The trick is treating Claude as a sparring partner that never gets tired and never gets defensive when it’s wrong.

But remember: it’s still just a hypothesis. The real work starts when you talk to actual buyers.

To get there fast, use Gojiberry to find and reach the right people to test it with.

Customer conversations are the only thing that matters.
