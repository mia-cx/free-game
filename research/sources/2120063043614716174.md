## 1. Pick the keywords that pay before you write a word

A better model writing pages for the wrong keyword just gets you to zero faster.

"What is a coffee table" brings visitors, and nobody who types it is buying.

"Oak coffee table" is typed by someone with their card in hand.

You don't need a tool to read intent, because Google already tested it. Search the keyword and look at what ranks:

- Blog posts, a definition box on top: informational. Skip it for now.
- Product pages, category pages with prices: commercial. That's your target.
Then the second filter, the one that kills most projects: can you beat this results page at your size?


```
Using ChatSEO, give me the best SEO strategy for my site. Then:

1. Keep only keywords where the current top 3 are product, category
   or landing pages. Drop anything blog posts dominate.
2. For each keyword left, tell me if sites my size rank in the top 10.
   If the top 10 are all big brands, mark it LATER.
3. Give me the 10 best keywords to start with: keyword, monthly
   volume, the page type Google wants.
4. Use only the volumes ChatSEO returns. Never estimate one.
```
Rule 4 is the line people skip. If the model gives you a search volume no tool gave it, it's guessing.

## 3. One keyword, one page, better than the top 3

The most expensive mistake is writing a page you already have.

Two pages chasing "oak coffee table" split Google's attention, and neither wins.

Before writing, run the check Paul does by hand: Search Console, Performance, filter on the exact search, then the Pages tab. If a page already shows up, you improve it instead of writing a new one.

Then switch to High and build the page from what already ranks.

```
Using ChatSEO, pull the top 3 results for "oak coffee table" and read
their pages.

1. List every section all 3 have. My page needs each one.
2. List what only 1 of them has. Say if it is worth copying.
3. Find what none of them have that I could add, using only what I
   can really show (photos of the grain, real dimensions, a care
   guide, delivery times).
4. Write the outline: one H1 with the keyword, H2s, what goes in each.
5. Then write the page. Mark every fact you could not verify as
   [CHECK].
```
Step 3 is the whole game. A page that only matches the top 3 is a tenth copy of the same page, and Google has nine already.

Anthropic says 5.2 communicates more clearly than 5.1. That makes the draft better, not finished.

Then you edit every line and every [CHECK]. Your name goes on the page, not the model's.

## 4. Check what Fable wrote before Google does

Most pages that don't rank fail on something a checklist would have caught.

The list, for the "oak coffee table" page:

- Title tag. The keyword near the start, under 60 characters, written for a click.
- Meta description. One sentence that says what the reader gets.
- One H1. With the keyword. Only one.
- URL. Short, the keyword, no dates.
- Internal links. At least 2 of your pages link to it, with words that describe it.
- Images. Compressed, with alt text that says what's in them.
- Structured data. Product, with price and availability.
Point Fable at the live URL and run this:

```
Read the HTML of [URL], my page targeting "oak coffee table".

Check it against this list and give me a pass/fail table:
title tag, meta description, single H1 with the keyword, URL,
internal links pointing in and out, image alt text, structured data.

For every fail, write the exact fix: the new title, the new meta,
the Product JSON-LD block ready to paste.
```
The JSON-LD is where the model earns its keep. Nobody writes structured data by hand twice.


### The sitemap trap

Your sitemap tells Google which pages exist.

Paul was testing a ChatSEO feature that reads a site's sitemap, and it kept ignoring a page he had published the day before. The page was live, but the sitemap had stopped updating.

Nothing warns you. Check that your newest page is in your sitemap after every publish.


## 5. Every Monday, let Search Console tell Fable what to fix

After a few weeks, Google tells you what to do next, and most people never ask.

Look for searches where you sit between positions 5 and 15. Google already thinks you're relevant, so you're one good edit from page one.

```
Using ChatSEO, look at my Search Console for the last 28 days.

1. List the searches where my average position is between 5 and 15,
   with clicks, impressions and the page that ranks.
2. Group them by page.
3. For the 3 pages with the most impressions, tell me what to add or
   change so the page answers those searches better.
4. Skip any page I edited in the last 60 days. Google hasn't
   re-read it yet.
```
Run it at Medium. It's reading, not writing.

One hour a week. That's the whole loop.
