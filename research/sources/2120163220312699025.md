### 4 things nobody tells you


1. About 90% of the advice is the same dozen ideas. Most of them are classic SEO.

2. Almost none of the big numbers come with data. In at least two articles, the "AI mentions" in the headline were impressions in Google Search Console. The article said so itself, further down.

3. The thresholds everyone quotes ("position 8 to 15", "5 internal links", "DR 20") are rules of thumb. The good sources say so. The others state them as facts.

4. GitHub stars don't tell you if the advice is current. One repo with 11,000 stars gets ChatGPT's crawler wrong. More on that below.

That doesn't make the advice bad. It means you can stop paying for it, and you should check it.



### 1. The foundation

Google and AI engines need to be able to read you.

- Open your page source. If your headline and main text aren't in the raw HTML, a crawler that doesn't run JavaScript sees an empty page.

- Read your robots.txt. GPTBot, ClaudeBot and Google-Extended are for training. OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and Bingbot power live answers. Block the second group and you vanish from AI search.

- Read the live file, not the one in your project. Cloudflare can add its own block of rules at the top, and it can block AI bots with one toggle.

- Test it: `curl -A "OAI-SearchBot" -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" https://yoursite.com/`. Anything but 200 is a problem.

- Add a sitemap with only indexable pages, a canonical tag on every page, and structured data (Organization, WebSite, and Product or SoftwareApplication). No review stars until you have real reviews.

I had Claude run these checks on my own site, before launch. Cloudflare had added nothing to my robots.txt. But I had no sitemap, no canonical tag, no structured data, and my www address didn't redirect to the main one.

I thought the site was ready.



### 2. Bing is back


Set up Bing Webmaster Tools. Import from Google Search Console, submit the sitemap, turn on IndexNow. Ten minutes.

Copilot runs on Bing, and ChatGPT has leaned on it for search. Bing also has an AI Performance report: the queries where AI answers cited your site. It's the closest thing to free, first-party citation data.



### 3. Build pages for decisions, not for keywords

The pages that get cited, and that convert, are the ones people read right before choosing: 

X vs Y, X alternatives, best X for one kind of person, X pricing.

The rules, with the numbers from the skills:

- Google the query first. If 6 or more of the top 10 are the same type of page, that's the type to build. If they're all blog posts and you sell a product, skip it.

- 2 keywords belong on the same page when they share 4 or more of their top 10 results.

- Answer in the first 40 to 80 words, and name your product. AI engines lift passages, not pages.

- A "best of" list has 5 to 8 options. 3 is an advert, 25 is a directory.

- Every price comes from the vendor's own page, marked "checked on" with a date. One real drawback per option, yours included.

- Don't rank yourself first in your own list.

- Add one thing the top 10 results don't have: your own number, your own test, your own screenshot. Show the method.



### 4. GEO: what's actually new

- Assistants run about ten hidden searches for every question. Open the "searching" step in ChatGPT or Claude and you can read them. Each one is a page you need, or a site you need to be on.

- Most citations go to third parties: "best of" lists, YouTube, review sites. In one vendor's dataset (44,070 citations, 10 brands), 85% went to third-party pages and under 4% to the brand's own site. But the brands whose own site got cited were named far more often. You need both.

- Answers move. One study quoted in these articles says ChatGPT swaps most of its sources from one week to the next. A single test proves nothing.

- AI gets facts about you wrong. Write a dated fact sheet (price, plans, platforms), ask the engines, and trace each wrong claim to the page it came from. Fix it there. Call it fixed only when it's gone two months in a row.

- If you sell software, search your "best X" query on a phone. If Google shows a row of videos, make the video. Long-form first, chapters from 00:00, captions corrected by hand.



### 5. Measure it for free

Skip the paid visibility trackers for now.

- Write 15 to 30 real buyer questions. Take them from your inbox, your sales calls and your reviews, not your imagination.

- Run each one at least twice per engine, logged out: ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini.

- Log who got named and which URLs got cited. Being cited and being recommended are two different numbers.

- Under about 100 AI visits a month, report counts, not percentages.

- Add "How did you hear about us?" at signup. One founder compared it with his analytics: the referrer credited Google with 3.4 times more signups than his customers did.

The free stack: Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, Ahrefs Webmaster Tools, Screaming Frog (500 URLs).



### 6. Links and mentions

Worth your time:

- Launch platforms and software directories. Same name, same one-line description, same logo everywhere. 20 to 30 of them, not 300.

- Unlinked mentions. Search `"your brand" -site:yoursite.com` and ask for the link.

- "Best of" lists that name your competitors but not you. A pitch of 120 to 180 words, with evidence. One follow-up after a week, then stop.

- A customer story about a tool you use. Vendors link to those.

- Reddit and Quora, as yourself, disclosed. One answer that mentions your company per subreddit per week, at most.


What gets you burned:

- Link exchanges and credit networks. The same articles that sell them admit Google calls it link spam.

- Paid Reddit and Quora "tasks", fake accounts, reviews you wrote yourself.

- 1,500 AI posts across a network of sites.

- City × service template pages.



### 7. If an AI agent does your SEO

- Numbers come from the tool, never from the model. Test it: ask for your top 5 pages by clicks and compare with Search Console.

- 3 permission tiers. 

Allowed: read data, write drafts. 

Needs approval: publish, change a title. 

Never on its own: delete a page, change robots.txt or a canonical. 

You see the diff first.

- Put the "never" in a hook, not in a prompt.

- Snapshot your key pages before every deploy and compare after. The four changes that kill a site quietly: a page turns noindex, a canonical points elsewhere, a 200 becomes a 404, robots.txt blocks everything.

- Grade every draft before it ships. The best skill I read scores 8 areas out of 5 and passes at 32 out of 40, with nothing under 3.



### 8. New domain? Slow down

- 1 to 4 new pages a week. The authors disagree on the number, not on the direction.

- Wait 2 to 3 months before judging a change.

- Most Search Console playbooks (striking distance, title tests, pruning) need data you don't have yet. Save them for month three.



### The two tools behind 49 of these articles

Two companies wrote most of what I read:

Everything in this playbook can be done by hand. These tools sell the three things that are slow, or close to impossible, by hand.

- Keyword data. Search volumes at scale cost money. ChatSEO pulls live volumes, backlinks and competitor data from DataForSEO, next to your Search Console (read-only). That's section 3 without Googling every query yourself.

- Tracking AI answers every day. Section 5 by hand is 30 questions, 4 engines, twice each. Distribb tracks when ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google's AI answers cite you.

- Writing and publishing. Both write articles and push them to your CMS: WordPress, Shopify and Webflow for ChatSEO, and those plus Wix, Ghost, Notion and Framer for Distribb.

Both plug into your AI agent through an MCP server: 

on every Distribb plan, from the Pro plan on ChatSEO. 

ChatSEO also crawls your site and gives you a prioritized fix list.

Prices: ChatSEO starts at €29 a month (€49 with the MCP), with a 7-day trial. Distribb is $97 a month, with a 3-day trial.

2 things I'd leave off on a new domain. 

Distribb has a backlink exchange you turn on with one toggle ("3 to 5 links a month, on autopilot"), and its plan writes 30 articles a month. Sections 6 and 8 say why.

When to pay: once the foundation is done and you know which pages you want. Not before.



### The 14 repos, sorted

A quick test for any SEO skill: ask it which bot decides whether you show up in ChatGPT search. OpenAI's own docs say OAI-SearchBot. GPTBot is for training.

Worth installing:

- RomeoSEO/seo-skills (48 skills, MIT). The clearest method, and nothing sold inside the files. https://github.com/RomeoSEO/seo-skills

- AgriciDaniel/claude-seo (27.8k stars, 26 skills). The most precise on technical checks, structured data and AI crawlers, with real scripts behind it. It gets the bot question right, and it says "Score only what was measured." Use the page, technical and schema commands. Know that it adds a link to a paid community after big reports. https://github.com/AgriciDaniel/claude-seo


Worth it once you have data:

- AminForou/mcp-gsc (2.2k). Connects Claude to your Search Console. Read-only by default, runs on your machine, free. https://github.com/AminForou/mcp-gsc

- every-app/open-seo (27.1k). An open-source alternative to Semrush and Ahrefs. Best line in it: "Search volume is not visits." Needs paid data. https://github.com/every-app/open-seo

- nowork-studio/notfair-plugin (4.1k, 45 skills). A solid page audit that works without Search Console data. It runs through their hosted server, and it still rates FAQ markup as "High Impact if Missing", years after Google cut FAQ rich results back to a handful of sites. https://github.com/nowork-studio/notfair-plugin


Take one idea, leave the rest:

- zubair-trabzada/geo-seo-claude (11.6k). Keep its Cloudflare check: read your live robots.txt for a block Cloudflare added. Leave the rest. Its crawler file says that if you block GPTBot your content "will NOT appear in ChatGPT Search results", which OpenAI's docs contradict. Its SaaS checklist still lists HowTo markup, which Google stopped showing in 2023. And it installs with curl piped into bash from the main branch, so read the script first. https://github.com/zubair-trabzada/geo-seo-claude

- TheCraigHewitt/seomachine (7.6k). A blog-writing pipeline. Keep the context files (brand voice, features, style guide). Drop the "1-2% density" target, and delete the line that asks for mini-stories with names "even if fictional". https://github.com/TheCraigHewitt/seomachine

- Ryze-AI-Adgent/open-seo-mcp-skills (9.1k). One great rule: "Never invent volumes." But the skills are short, and your data goes through their hosted connector. https://github.com/Ryze-AI-Adgent/open-seo-mcp-skills

- i365dev/awesome-seo (2.9k). A current, vendor-free reading list. Best tip: Cloudflare's Bot Fight Mode can challenge legitimate crawlers, so check your security events after launch. https://github.com/i365dev/awesome-seo


One more thing. 3 of these repos give your site a score out of 100. 

The weights were never checked against real rankings or real citations. 

Read the findings. Ignore the number.

### Hand this to your AI

You don't have to do this by hand. Copy the prompt below, fill in the three lines, then paste this whole article under it.

It works best with an agent that can open your site's project (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex). A chat assistant can run the audit and write the drafts, not make the fixes.


Apply the playbook below to my site.

Site: [your URL]

Product: [one sentence]

Competitors: [2 or 3 names]


Work in this order. Stop after each step and show me what you found.


1. Audit only. Change nothing. Fetch my live robots.txt, the raw HTML of my homepage, my sitemap and my www address. For every point in section 1, say pass or fail, with the proof: the line or the status code.

2. List the fixes, smallest first. For each one: the file, the change, and how you'll check it worked.

3. Wait for my yes before each change. Show me the diff first for anything that touches robots.txt, canonicals, redirects or noindex. Never delete a page.

4. After each change, run the check again and show me the result.

5. Give me the list of what only I can do (Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, directories, outreach, videos), in order.

6. Draft 20 buyer questions for my product. Mark the ones you guessed, so I can replace them with real ones.

7. Propose 5 decision pages from section 3. For each: the query, what the top 10 results look like today, and the one thing my page would add. Don't write any page until I pick one.


Rules:

- A number comes from a tool or a page you fetched, never from memory. If you can't check something, write "not checked".

- Never invent a search volume.

- No review stars, no FAQ markup, no HowTo markup.

- Install no skill or repo from this article without showing me the file first.
