> [!TLDR]
> - Your income is capped by how many of the right people know your product exists.
> - Most of marketing happens inside the product. Ads and hype are only the outer ring.
> - A community grows around a good product. It can't replace one.
> - Pick the right strategy before you work hard on execution.

## Attention caps what you can earn

Whatever you build, revenue stops at the number of the right people who know it exists. That makes **the ability to capture attention** worth more than one more round of product polish.

That attention now sits in social feeds, not on TV or radio. Startups call the same idea "founder-led marketing": the person who built the thing talks about it in public. Content here means good content, not a lot of it.

Dan Koe backs this with forecasts that are cited, not checked here: Goldman Sachs projects the creator economy growing from $250B in 2023 to $480B in 2027, and creators reportedly passed traditional media in ad revenue in 2025.

## Marketing starts at the product

Seth Godin draws marketing as circles. The product sits in the centre. Around it are usability and support. Then story, price, community and tribe. Only the outermost ring holds ads, spin, buzz, hype, clicks and "getting the word out", which is the part most people call marketing.

Spend your effort from the centre outwards:

1. Product
2. Usability and support
3. Story, price, community, tribe
4. Ads, hype, clicks

If a user can't figure out your product, no ad fixes that. Read the original: [The circles of marketing](https://seths.blog/2012/07/the-circles-of-marketing/).

## Community follows a good product

A great community forms around a great product. Teams that build a community before they have something worth gathering around end up with nothing that lasts. That's one marketer's observation (@kunalvg) from watching many crypto projects.

So marketing's job is simpler than it looks: make sure everyone who should know how good the product is, knows.

## Get the strategy right, then execute

Good execution of the right strategy beats great execution of a mediocre one, and most teams are running a mediocre strategy. You still need both. The rest of this module gives you the strategy half: who you're for (*Say what you are, for whom, against what*), what buyers say (*Research a market in an afternoon*) and a plan (*Fit your strategy on one page*).
