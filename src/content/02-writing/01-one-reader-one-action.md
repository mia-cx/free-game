---
title: Write for one reader and one action
summary: Decide who a post is for, what they'll do and what they'll take away, before writing.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - Copywriting means getting the right person to take the right action. Pick the action first.
> - One idea per post. Three ideas are three posts.
> - Keep the idea advanced and the words simple enough for a 12-year-old.
> - Tell the change your product makes, from the user's point of view.

## Pick the action before you write

Copywriting is getting the right person to take the right action: read, reply, share, follow or buy. So **decide the action before you write the first word**. Everything else in the post serves that one action.

The "right person" is the reader you pictured in *Say what you are, for whom, against what*. The action gets its own lesson: *Ask for one thing at the end*.

## Keep one idea per post

A post with one idea is easier to remember and easier to pass on. It also helps the feed: X shows posts to clusters of people with shared interests (its SimClusters system), and a post about three topics confuses which cluster it belongs to. That's one author's reading of X's published code plus their own experience. *How X picks what people see* covers the feed in detail.

Every post needs three things: a clear takeaway, a specific reader and a specific call to action. If you have three ideas, you have three posts.

## Choose clarity over cleverness

Good writing removes friction. Use vocabulary a 12-year-old can read, even when the idea is advanced. Keep the concept intact; simplify the words and the formatting.

Before you post, ask: "What does someone walk away with?" If you can't say it in a sentence, the post isn't ready.

## Write for the layperson and the expert

Picture a magician doing a trick nobody has ever done. A non-magician says "whoa, cool." The magician sitting next to them loses their mind, because they know it's never been done.

Aim for that. Write something a layperson finds interesting and an expert finds ten times more so. Copy written for a general audience still lands with experts, but copy written only for experts loses everyone else. Check each draft: would a non-specialist stop for this?

## Tell the change from the user's side

Storytelling only works when the product makes a real change in someone's life. Find the focal point of that change, the moment things are different, and tell it through the user's eyes. The user is the main character; the product is what helped.

> [!DO] Write a three-line post plan
> Take your next post idea and write three lines above the draft: "Reader: ___", "Action: ___" (one of read, reply, share, follow, buy) and "Takeaway: ___" (one sentence). Cut anything in the draft that doesn't serve those three.

> [!CHECK] In copywriting, what three things does every post need?

> [!CHECK] Your draft post covers three separate ideas. What do you do?
