> [!TLDR]
> - A hook has one job: get the next line read.
> - Open a gap the reader wants closed, and make it about them.
> - Most hooks are one of eight types, or a mix.
> - Write the hook last, in 5 to 10 versions, then cut.

## A hook gets the next line read

The first line doesn't have to sell anything. Its only job is to **get the second line read**. It does that by opening a gap the reader wants closed and making it about them.

- Lead with the most interesting thing. Cut the warm-up. If your best line is at the bottom, move it to the top.
- Be specific. "I hit 5.8M impressions in my first month" beats "I grew fast". Numbers, names and timeframes beat vague promises.
- Answer "what's in it for me?" Keep it short. Create tension with a question, a surprise or a contradiction.

## Pull at least one lever

Every working hook pulls at least one of five levers: self-interest, curiosity, novelty, emotion (surprise, fear, FOMO, excitement) or identity ("people like me"). The strongest stack two or three.

Example: "If you're a founder under 1K followers, this one change doubled my reach." The first half is identity. The second is self-interest plus curiosity.

> [!TRICK] Pick from eight hook types
> 1. Bold claim: "Copywriting is the most underrated skill on 𝕏."
> 2. Callout: "If you're a founder with under 1K followers, read this."
> 3. Curiosity gap: "I just realized something 99% of founders miss."
> 4. Clear outcome: "How to write posts like the top 1%."
> 5. Contrarian: "F\*ck posting volume. You NEED to post less."
> 6. Story: "It took me 8 years to realize this..."
> 7. Number or list: "7 copywriting rules I wish I knew 6 years ago."
> 8. Proof: "This post got 2M views. I analyzed why (takeaways)."
>
> The author reports 1B+ lifetime impressions on X, and +9,000 followers and about 6M impressions in the first month of a new account. That's their own account, not a study.

## Put the hook where the format needs it

A hook isn't always the first sentence. In a video it's the first two seconds; in an article it's three separate pieces. Match it to the format.

> [!TRICK] Fit the hook to the format
> ```
> Short posts   → The hook IS the post. Make line one strong enough to stand alone.
> Threads       → Hook + promise of what's coming ("Here are 7 lessons:")
> Articles      → Title + subtitle + first paragraph all need to hook. Treat each as its own hook.
> Video         → The first 1 to 2 seconds. Visual movement + text on screen + spoken hook.
> Quote posts   → Your take needs to add tension or value to the original, not just "this."
> Images        → The headline on the image is the hook. One idea, readable at phone size.
> ```

## Write the hook last

You don't know what the post promises until it's written. So write the body first, then write many hooks and cut down to one.

> [!TRICK] Write 5 to 10 hooks, keep one
> 1. Write the post first, hook last.
> 2. Write 5 to 10 versions using different hook types.
> 3. Cut the weakest immediately.
> 4. Refine each survivor until every line rolls into the next.
> 5. Cut words that repeat or add nothing.

> [!DO] Write five hooks for one post
> Take a post you've drafted or already published. Write five new first lines for it, each using a different type from the list of eight. Cut to the one you'd stop scrolling for, and save all five in a doc.
