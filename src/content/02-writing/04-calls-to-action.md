---
title: Ask for one thing at the end
summary: End each post with one low-effort ask that fits how well the reader knows you.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - Readers tend to do what you ask, so ask for one thing.
> - Match the ask to how well they know you: strangers follow or share, fans reply or click, warm readers buy.
> - Keep the ask low effort, and don't save it only for the end.

## Readers do what you ask

Readers tend to do what a post tells them to do. So close every post with **one low-effort ask**: the action you chose in *Write for one reader and one action*.

> [!TRICK] Use a call to action for each action
> ```
> Reply   → "Which one do you disagree with?"
> Share   → "Send this to a friend who's building a brand."
> Follow  → "Follow for daily posts on copywriting."
> Save    → "Bookmark this for your next post."
> Click   → "Full breakdown in the article below."
> Buy     → "DM me 'system' and I'll send you the details."
> ```

## Match the ask to the funnel stage

Ask for what fits how well the reader knows you. Asking a stranger for a sale is a big jump; asking for a follow is a small one. These stages are the top, middle and bottom of the funnel from *Map the whole funnel*.

| Stage | Who they are | What to ask for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top of funnel | They don't know you yet | A follow or a share |
| Middle of funnel | They trust you a bit | A reply, a save or a click |
| Bottom of funnel | They're ready | The sale, a DM or a sign-up |

## Place the ask where people still are

The end of a post isn't the only spot for an ask.

- Place a call to action early too, where the most readers are still reading.
- Post your call to action as a reply under your best-performing post.
- Ask readers for something: their advice, or what they want you to write next.

> [!DO] Add one ask to your next post
> Decide whether your next post is for strangers, regulars or warm readers. Pick the matching ask from the table, write it as the last line, and add the same line as a reply under your best-performing post.

> [!CHECK] In a post's call to action, what should you ask readers who don't know you yet?
> A follow or a share. A sale is too big a jump for a stranger.

> [!CHECK] Your post is for warm readers who already trust you and are ready. What do you ask for at the end?
> The sale, a DM or a sign-up. Readers at the bottom of the funnel are ready for the bigger ask.

> [!CHECK] Why should a post's call to action sometimes go early instead of only at the end?
> More people are still reading early in a post than at the end, so an early ask reaches more of them.
