> [!TLDR]
> - One founder reached 5,000 followers in 5 months with one long X article a day, on one topic, written to be bookmarked.
> - Make the cover, title and first line tell one story, with a number up front.
> - Post as often as each post still has its own job; when reach per post drops, you're flooding.
> - Paste long guides into an AI assistant and ask how they apply to your account.

## One topic, one article a day

Nicholas Dulait (@NicholasDulait) crossed 5,000 X followers in 5 months by publishing **one long article every day on one topic**, then using short posts to send people to it. The post is a bare checklist with no breakdown of what drove the growth, so treat it as one person's recipe to test.

The pieces fit the rest of this module: one topic keeps you inside one interest community (see *How X picks what people see*), and quotes of your articles are among the heavier-weighted actions.

> [!TRICK] Follow the daily article checklist
> - Pick one topic. Build in public, share real revenue and lead with proof.
> - Publish one long article every day, written for bookmarks.
> - Write on fresh trends, and give everything away.
> - Post 2 to 3 times a day. Quote your own articles, and get someone else to quote them.
> - Prefer long posts over one-liners.
> - Double down on what wins, and don't stop publishing.

Nicholas's list also says "make broad promises". Ryan's copywriting advice says the opposite: "Numbers, names, and timeframes always beat vague promises." Where they clash, stay specific, as *Make the first line earn the second* teaches.

## Make cover, title and hook one story

An article shows up in the feed as a cover image, a title and a first line. They should tell one consistent story.

> [!TRICK] Package the article
> - Copy the cover styles of articles that already win.
> - Write titles that hook hard, and put numbers in the first line.
> - Add screenshots as proof.
> - Keep links out of the post body.

"Keep links out" is a common belief, but Nicholas gives no data for it. Ryan's weight table gives opening a link a small positive weight (0.2), not a penalty. If a link helps the reader, test it.

## How much to post

The sources disagree. Ryan says that if you post too much, X doesn't give every post a chance, and argues for posting less. Nicholas posts 2 to 3 times a day plus an article. Neither gives a number.

Use this rule: every post needs its own job. A daily article plus a few posts that quote it or share one idea from it is fine, because each one sends people to the same piece. Watch reach per post, not total reach. If adding posts makes each one reach fewer people, cut back to the ones that pull.

## Let AI digest the long guides

Platform guides like the ones this module draws on run to thousands of words. Ryan's suggestion: tag Grok on the post, or paste the guide into any assistant, and ask what the algorithm rewards, the best practices, and how to increase your reach. Then ask how it applies to your account and your last few posts.

> [!DO] Write ten titles
> Pick your one topic and write 10 article titles for it, each with a number in the first line. Save them as your next two weeks of articles.

> [!CHECK] On X, what three parts of an article show in the feed and should tell one story?
> The cover image, the title and the first line.

> [!CHECK] You add more posts each day on X and each post now reaches fewer people. What do you do?
> Cut back to the posts that pull. Falling reach per post means you're flooding, so every post needs its own job.

> [!CHECK] In a daily X article routine, why stick to one topic?
> One topic keeps you inside one interest community, so X knows who to show your articles to.
