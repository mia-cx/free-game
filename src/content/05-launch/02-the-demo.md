title: Show the product at its extreme
summary: Plan a launch demo that shows your product's biggest feat and proves it's real.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - The narrative gets people to stop. The demo decides whether they believe you.
> - Show the most extreme thing your product can do, in a way nobody can dismiss as fake.
> - Video is the medium launches benefit from most. Learn it whatever platform you post on.

## The demo earns belief

A good narrative makes someone stop scrolling (see *Give strangers a reason to care*). Then the demo has a different job: make the viewer think "this is real". If it looks staged or vague, the stop is wasted.

## Push the product to its extreme

**Show the most extreme thing your product can do**, then make the proof impossible to wave away.

Matt Epstein uses the Tesla Roadster launch as an example. The car drives up a curved ramp, hangs upside down, flips through the air, lands on its wheels, then jumps over a whole row of Teslas. Jumping one car is interesting; jumping the fleet is something you send to ten friends. And it was done live, in front of a crowd, on a livestream. Filmed any other way, viewers would have assumed CGI.

You don't need a stunt budget. You need two answers.

> [!TRICK] Answer two questions, then film only those moments
> 1. What is the most extreme thing your product can do?
> 2. How do you make people believe it happened?
>
> Film exactly the moments that answer both. For software, show the real input, the real output and anything you fixed by hand (the spreadsheet-to-app example in *Give strangers a reason to care*).

## Learn video as a medium

Kunal Gandhi argues you should master video as a way to communicate, whatever platform it lands on. The skill is knowing what captures your audience's imagination on screen. In Gandhi's experience, product previews, demo videos and quirky humour outperform everything else, and launches gain the most from video. The post points to thirdweb's launch videos as a model.

Start with a screen recording of the extreme moment. When you want it to look premium, see *Make product videos feel premium*.

> [!DO] Write your demo's shot list
> Write your answers to the two questions, then list the three moments you would film to prove them, one line each.
