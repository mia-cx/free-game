title: Line up your launch-day voices
summary: Build a list of people who will quote your launch, each with their own angle.

> [!TLDR]
> - Ask people with an audience to quote your launch, not retweet it. A quote is a new post to their followers.
> - Line everyone up to post at the same moment, and tie the launch to an ecosystem people already watch.
> - Seed different angles (founder story, implications, a sceptical try-out) so each quote reaches a different crowd.
> - Pick a few creators who fit over many who are big, and brief them so they write their own take.

> [!DIAGRAM] launch-timeline

## Ask for quotes, not retweets

A follower can see you. A connection has an audience and will answer your text. **When a connection quotes your launch, it becomes a new post from them**, shown to their followers. A retweet does far less.

Keep the launch in one post so every quote points to the same place. X shows one post per conversation, so a launch split across five posts loses most of them (see *How X picks what people see*).

Matt Epstein frames the ask with a story from Opendoor co-founder JD Ross: asking someone powerful for an intro costs a favour and gets you one meeting. Asking them to quote your launch costs the same favour and reaches their whole audience.

> [!TRICK] Build and tier your quote list
> 1. Export the followers of everyone on your team who has an account.
> 2. Add the people no export shows: investors, advisors, customers, beta testers, portfolio founders.
> 3. Rank everyone by how far they reach into your customer.
> 4. The top ~25 get a personal message from the founder.
>
> - Bad: "Hey, we're launching Tuesday, would love a retweet!"
> - Good: "Hey, here's what we've been working on. Would you quote this? Here's an example of a great one from an investor on a similar launch."
>
> Epstein's agency claims 200M views across 30+ X launches and 2,000+ booked demos, with one launch (WithCoverage) booking 600. These are the agency's own numbers, unverified, and it sells launch services.

## Post together, on a story people already follow

Kunal Gandhi calls this a **thunderclap**: founders, friends and partners all post about the launch at the same set time. Line up the list in advance.

Then attach your message to conversations already trending rather than fighting them. Anchor the launch to the platform or ecosystem you build on, and use your integrations as the onboarding story. In Gandhi's experience, getting bigger players in your ecosystem to notice you beats paid campaigns.

## Seed different angles

Each angle reaches a different audience, so plan the takes others could have before launch day.

> [!TRICK] Five quote angles to seed
> - **Founder story:** why they built it, a personal problem or an unusual path.
> - **Implications:** which part of someone's work changes, who benefits, why a feature matters more than it looks.
> - **Video reaction:** someone tries it on camera, pauses on the interesting parts, says where they're sceptical.
> - **Controversial take:** one specific implication someone will defend, with enough evidence to be challenged.
> - **Competing interests:** who saves money and whose business is threatened; who gains independence and who has to maintain it.

> [!RISK] Controversy sticks to the product
> A controversial angle shapes what people believe about your product long after launch. Only invite disagreement your team can answer with evidence.

## Pick creators for fit, not reach

Gandhi's warning: mediocre influencers deliver numbers that turn into nothing. A few well-matched ones shape the right narrative and bring the right audience.

Fit means they live with the problem and their audience would plausibly try the product. Read their posts and replies first: who pays attention, what questions come up? Then give them real access and let them write their own take. If every creator posts the same paragraph, you've lost the point.

> [!TRICK] Brief a creator in five lines
> - Here's what the product does.
> - Here's what you can test yourself.
> - This is the part I think your audience would care about.
> - Here's the evidence behind the claim.
> - What's your take after trying it?
>
> Offer a call if they need help understanding it.

> [!RISK] Undisclosed paid posts
> Paid posts without disclosure break platform rules and advertising law in many countries. Disclose every paid partnership.

> [!DO] Draft your top-25 quote list
> In a spreadsheet, list 25 people who could quote your next launch, with one column for their reach into your customer and one for the angle you'd ask them to take.
