> [!TLDR]
> - Before launch, decide who answers product questions and who follows up on leads.
> - Answer in the thread. DM only with a clear reason.
> - Log every question, objection and surprise use in one shared doc.
> - Judge the launch by trials, relevant DMs and sales calls, not views, then run a second wave within 1 to 2 weeks.

## Split the replies before you post

A launch creates replies and quotes in many places at once: your posts, other people's quotes, and creators' comment sections. **Assign owners before launch**: who answers product questions, who follows up on business leads.

Answer where people asked, in the thread. Send a DM only when you have a clear reason and something relevant to what they said, and check their profile first. A like on its own tells you nothing.

## Keep one doc of what people ask

What people say after a launch tells you what to fix, explain and post next. Capture it in one shared doc so it outlives the week.

> [!TRICK] Turn the launch thread into assets
> 1. Log recurring questions, objections, product issues and unexpected use cases, with links and the answer you gave.
> 2. Repeated questions become the FAQ. Features that confuse people get a walkthrough. Feedback goes to someone who can act on it.
> 3. When you ship a fix someone asked for, go back and tell them.
> 4. Ask permission to feature people's tests and results, credit them, and use them as your next posts.

## Count conversations, not views

Views tell you a post travelled. They don't tell you it worked. Measure a launch by the **trials, relevant DMs and sales calls** it produced.

When a prospect's source is unclear, ask them where they found you. Track only what you can verify.

## Run a second wave

The first wave tells you who brought useful conversations and which use case buyers keep raising. Use both.

- Rebook creators who brought useful conversations within 1 to 2 weeks, with something new: a deeper walkthrough, a customer use case, or an update that answers first-round questions.
- Give the use case buyers keep raising more space in the next demo and on the landing page.

> [!TRICK] Pre-launch checklist
> - A clear claim with evidence.
> - Different angles for different sharers.
> - Creators who understand the product and its audience.
> - A team ready to answer and follow up.
> - An easy way to try the product or get in touch.

All of this comes from one launch agency founder's practice (0xfJuan, Ark Media), not measured data.

> [!DO] Set up the launch log
> Create a shared doc with five columns: question or comment, link, type (question, objection, issue, use case), answer given, owner. Add the first row from any reply you've had this month.
