> [!TLDR]
> - When other platforms rank for your query, publish a complete answer there and link back to your site.
> - Where Google shows videos, publish a demo of your product doing that exact task.
> - Use Google Trends to answer rising questions before anyone else does.

## Borrow a site Google already trusts

A new site takes months to earn rankings. **Some platforms already have them.** If you search your target query and see LinkedIn articles, Medium posts or forum threads on page one, Google is happy to rank that platform for it. Publish a complete answer there and link to your site as the next step. This is sometimes called parasite SEO.

The rules are simple. Put the topic in the headline and the answer in the opening. Write for that platform's readers, not as a copy of your blog post. Link to one relevant next step, not five.

> [!TRICK] Rank a LinkedIn article
> - Pick queries LinkedIn already ranks for.
> - Put the query early in the headline. The headline sets the article's URL, so check it before you publish.
> - Lead with the answer and an example.
> - Link to the LinkedIn article from your own site.

## Make videos where Google shows videos

For some questions, Google puts videos on the results page. **Those are openings for a product demo.** Search your buyer questions and note which ones show video results.

> [!TRICK] Publish a demo for a video search
> 1. Confirm videos appear for the query.
> 2. Pick one specific product question.
> 3. Record the product completing that exact task.
> 4. Name the task in the video title.
> 5. Add captions and chapters.
> 6. Answer related buyer questions in the description.
> 7. Embed the video next to the written answer on your site.

## Answer rising searches early

When something changes in your field, new questions appear before anyone has written good answers. **Being early is the advantage.** Explain the news through your own expertise rather than repeating it.

> [!TRICK] Ride a rising search with Google Trends
> 1. Filter Google Trends by your category and country.
> 2. Check the history to tell seasonal demand from a real spike.
> 3. Read the original announcement, not the coverage of it.
> 4. Explain what changed and what it means for your buyers.
> 5. Send your finding to reporters who cover the topic.

For reshaping one piece of research into each platform's own format, see *Turn one conversation into ten posts*.

> [!DO] Find one platform that ranks for you
> Search your three most important buyer questions in a private window and list every third-party platform (LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit, Medium) on page one. Pick one and note the headline you'd publish there.

> [!CHECK] Before you write about a rising search in Google Trends, what do you check?
> Its history, to tell seasonal demand from a real spike. Then read the original announcement, not the coverage of it.

> [!CHECK] Google shows videos on the results page for one of your buyer questions. What do you make?
> A video of your product completing that exact task, with the task named in the title. Embed it next to the written answer on your site.

> [!CHECK] When your own site is new, why publish answers on platforms like LinkedIn or Medium?
> A new site takes months to earn rankings, and those platforms already rank for your query. Your answer there links back to your site as the next step.
