---
title: Know what a link is worth
summary: Score a link prospect, then aim it at a page it can actually move.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - A link is worth chasing when it reaches your buyers, you genuinely qualify, and a real person could click it.
> - Domain Rating (DR) is a tiebreaker, not the reason.
> - Point your few links at pages already ranking 10 to 30. One SEO reports 2 to 5 links a month can lift them to page one.
> - Google treats mass directory submissions and automated links as spam.

## Judge a link by who could click it

**A link is a path for a person before it is a signal for Google.** Ask whether people who could buy from you visit that site, and whether they would ever click your link there.

A useful listing, a tool people keep referencing, or a real editorial mention outweighs a hundred placeholder pages that exist only to hold a URL. High DR on a site your buyers never visit is worth little.

## Score every prospect on five things

Before you spend time on a site, score it on these. If it fails relevance or eligibility, stop there, whatever its DR.

| Check | Question to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Relevance | Does this site cover your topic or your buyers' problem? |
| Eligibility | Do you genuinely qualify for it (product type, location, stage)? |
| Cost | What does it take in money or hours? |
| Placement | Once accepted, does the page actually show your URL, and is it clickable? |
| Audience | Do real visitors arrive here who could become customers? |

Placement catches a common trap: some sites accept your listing but show no link, or restrict clickable links to paid plans. Check a live listing before you submit.

## Aim links at pages close to page one

You will only earn a few links, so spend them where they move something. Pages Google already ranks a little are the best targets: Google has decided they are relevant, they just need a push. A brand-new page with no rankings gets far less from the same link.

> [!TRICK] Send links to your page-two pages
> 1. In Google Search Console, open Performance and look at pages by average position.
> 2. Pick the pages sitting at positions 10 to 30 (bottom of page one to page three).
> 3. Point new links at those pages, not your home page.
> 4. Aim for 2 to 5 links a month. Free sources count: directories, podcast guest spots, partner pages, answering journalist requests.
>
> One agency owner's report, not a measured study. Before linking, make the page itself complete: see *Improve the pages that already rank*.

> [!RISK] Mass submission is link spam
> Google's spam policies name low-quality directory links, automated link creation and manipulative link schemes as link spam. Use backlink lists to find a few places that fit, never as a batch to submit everywhere.

> [!DO] Pick your five link targets
> Open Search Console, find five pages ranking between positions 10 and 30, and paste their URLs into a doc titled "Link targets". If your site isn't in Search Console yet, set it up first (see *Get your site found at all*).
