---
title: Make product videos feel premium
summary: Set fixed rules for design, motion and music so every video looks deliberate.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - Premium means nothing looks accidental. Set fixed rules (3 colours, the same backgrounds, the same kind of music) and keep them.
> - Design and storyboard every frame before anyone animates.
> - Ease the motion, flow between scenes instead of cutting, and vary the pace.
> - Pick music by tempo for the feeling you want, then delete any sound effect that doesn't help.

## Premium comes from restraint

A video feels premium when **every choice looks deliberate**. Font, background, music and colour each need a reason. When they never wander, viewers assume someone decided it should be that way.

Apple's product videos are the model here. They aren't trying to make more people know Apple. They make people who already chose Apple feel special for it. Most cheap-looking videos fail by adding things: too many effects, unnecessary sound effects, hype music.

The source is @leomeethewoo, whose studio sells product videos to fintech companies and made 50+ of them in one summer. The rules below are his team's practice, not tested claims.

> [!TRICK] Write fixed rules per video type
> For each type of video you make (launch, feature, explainer):
> - Use the same 3 colours.
> - Use the same backgrounds.
> - Use similar music for similar projects.
>
> Write the rules down once and apply them to every video of that type.

## Design every frame before you animate

Quality is set at the design stage, not by hiring a great motion designer later. Apple keeps a brand guideline for typography, backgrounds and more, and sticks to it from video to video. Leo's team storyboards every animation frame by frame in Figma before any motion work starts.

Check each frame against four principles:

- One shot, one idea.
- Every scene has room to breathe.
- The key object sits in the centre of the frame.
- The background follows your brand guideline and never competes with the key object.

The centring rule clashes with the off-centre 38% / 62% rule in *Plan an AI video before you pay for it*. Use centring when the product is the subject, and the off-centre rule when people carry the story.

## Make the motion look expensive

Three animation habits separate expensive-looking motion from cheap.

> [!TRICK] Ease, flow and vary the pace
> 1. **Smoothness:** ease your keyframe graphs and overlap them. Linear motion reads as cheap and out of place.
> 2. **Dynamic transitions:** let one scene flow into the next so it feels like it belongs there. Use a hard cut only when the message needs one.
> 3. **Adaptive rhythm:** don't keep one pace. Speed up, slow down or hold, depending on the section.

## Choose the music by tempo

Tempo sets the energy of the whole video, including whether it feels premium. Pick the BPM first, then a genre that suits your audience.

> [!TRICK] Pick the BPM for the feeling
> | BPM | Feeling |
> | --- | --- |
> | 60–80 | regal, cinematic, heritage |
> | 90–110 | smooth, cool, effortless |
> | 115–123 | elite, kinetic, sophisticated |
> | faster | drive and hype: fits some projects, ruins others |
>
> Then do a sound pass: listen to the whole video and remove any effect that is too loud, out of place, or doesn't help people stay engaged or understand the product.

These BPM ranges are the author's opinion, but they give you a starting point instead of scrolling a music library at random.

> [!DO] Write your video rulebook
> Open a doc and write the rules for your next product video: 3 colours (hex codes), 1 background, 1 font, and a BPM range from the table. Keep it next to your editor and check every frame against it.
