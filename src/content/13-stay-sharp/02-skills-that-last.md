---
title: Pick skills AI makes more valuable
summary: Choose skills that AI amplifies, and keep the ones you need to supervise it.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - Learn skills where AI makes a person who knows a field faster. Skills AI can do alone are losing value.
> - Run any skill through three questions: do people pay for it, does AI speed you up rather than replace you, does it need field knowledge?
> - Be broad across channels and deep in the field you sell into.
> - Practise on purpose the skills you need to check AI's work, because they fade first.

## Learn skills AI amplifies

The skills that pay are the ones where AI makes someone who already knows a field faster. Skills AI can do without you are losing value. Run any skill you're considering through **three questions**:

1. Do people already pay for this result today?
2. Does AI make you faster at it, instead of doing it without you?
3. Does it need knowledge of a field, like sales, health or law?

If it passes all three, it's worth learning. The author, @notkvtwts, cites these studies to back the split (not checked here):

- PwC, across over a billion job ads: jobs asking for AI skills pay 62% more and grew 69%, against 9% for all jobs.
- MIT: writing tasks done 40% faster and 18% better with AI.
- Stanford, 2025: workers aged 22 to 25 in jobs AI can do alone dropped 13% relative to other jobs.
- Lightcast: over half of job ads asking for AI skills are outside tech.

## Cover every channel at a working level

On a small team with a tight budget, you are the marketing function. Kunal Gandhi, who built a marketing function at a data and AI startup through to its acquisition, lists what that takes: copywriting, SEO, performance marketing, community, content and go-to-market.

You don't need mastery in each. You need enough to run it yourself. This course covers each at that level, so revisit the modules you skimmed.

## Know your field well enough to talk shop

Breadth gets the work done. **Depth in your field** makes the strategy land. Gandhi says to learn the technology: not enough to code, but past sounding lost with builders. Gandhi credits months of reading and talking to experts with changing how those strategies got designed.

- Spend months reading and talking to experts in the field.
- Use the product yourself and feel every friction point.

A Uniswap marketer Gandhi quotes says the market is moving from attention playbooks to marketers like that, and sketches the profile: part marketer, part ecosystem and partner person, real industry knowledge, a decent writer, using AI tools to fill the gaps. Out of favour: agency veterans running old playbooks on products they don't use. Both write about crypto, but Gandhi says the advice applies outside it.

These two don't conflict. Rule: go broad across channels, go deep in the one field your buyers work in.

## Keep the skills supervision needs

Skills you stop using fade faster than you expect, and checking AI's work needs exactly those skills. Anthropic's research on its own staff calls this the paradox of supervision: "supervising Claude requires the very coding skills that may atrophy from AI overuse."

When you hit something beyond your understanding, learn it properly instead of pasting in a one-line explanation. For how to tell when you've stopped checking, see *Use AI without surrendering your judgment*.

> [!TRICK] Run a weekly calibration task
> From Addy Osmani's routine:
>
> - **Weekly:** do one real task without an agent, one that would hurt to forget how to do.
> - **Monthly:** write down what you've stopped doing, and decide which of those you're fine letting go.
> - **Anytime:** ask yourself how lost you'd be if AI didn't exist.

> [!DO] Score three skills
> Write a three-row table in a note: three skills you're considering, with yes or no for each of the three questions. Circle the one that gets three yeses.
