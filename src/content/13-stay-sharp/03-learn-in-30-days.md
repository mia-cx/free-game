title: Learn a new skill in 30 days
summary: Pick two AI skills, build on real work for 30 days, and judge it at 3 months.
---

> [!TLDR]
> - Pick two of five AI skills that fit your goal. Learn them on real problems, not courses alone.
> - Spend 30 days building one thing, using it on real work and showing it to 3 people. Judge the result at 3 months.
> - Audit your week to find tasks AI can draft. The paid part is the last 20%: choosing and fixing.
> - Have an AI interview you and write your plan, then report back to the same chat weekly.

## Pick two skills that fit your goal

Two skills that complement each other beat one skill alone. @notkvtwts names five to choose from: AI workflow design, context engineering, AI automation and agents, AI-assisted development, and content and distribution.

| Your goal | Pair these |
| --- | --- |
| A job you like | Workflow design + context engineering |
| Freelance work | Automation and agents + content and distribution |
| Your own product | AI-assisted development + content and distribution |
| Not sure yet | Context engineering, then add one more |

**Don't get attached to a tool.** Tools change every few months; the skill carries over. To check a skill is worth it before you start, run the test in *Pick skills AI makes more valuable*.

> [!TRICK] Run a 30-day build-and-share plan
> - **Week 1:** pick one skill and one real problem you have.
> - **Week 2:** build version 1: an assistant, an agent or an app.
> - **Week 3:** use it on real work and log the time it saves.
> - **Week 4:** share how you built it, and offer it to 3 people.
>
> Then keep going, and judge it only after 3 months.

## Find the tasks AI should draft

AI does roughly 80% of a task fast. The skill people pay for is the last 20%: choosing, fixing and catching mistakes. The author's example: AI writes 50 ads in a minute, and the skill is knowing which 3 to test and why the other 47 won't work.

> [!TRICK] Audit your week in three groups
> 1. List every task you do in a normal week.
> 2. Sort each into one of three groups: AI does it alone, AI drafts and you fix, AI can't help yet.
> 3. Set up an AI assistant for each task in the middle group (see *Give the bot your context, not clever prompts*).
> 4. Time each task before and after.
> 5. Write down the change, for example "used to take 4 hours, now 1". This log is also your proof that you can do it for others.

## Let an AI write your plan

Instead of hunting for a course, have an AI interview you and build a week-by-week plan for one skill. Fill in the brackets, paste it, and answer its questions. Save the chat and come back to it every week with what you did, so the plan adjusts. The author reports no results beyond their own use.

> [!TRICK] Paste the learning-plan prompt
> ```
> I want to get really good at [the skill] so I can [your goal, e.g.
> "get a better-paid job", "sell AI agents to small businesses",
> "launch my own product"] within [time, e.g. 3 months].
>
> WHERE I AM NOW
> - what I do now: [your job or studies]
> - how I use AI today: [e.g. "ChatGPT every day for writing and research,
>   never built an automation"]
> - what I already know about this skill: [be specific]
> - tools I already use: [e.g. Claude, Notion, Figma]
> - time I can spend: [hours per week]
> - budget for courses and tools: [amount, or "free only"]
> - how I learn best: [videos / reading / building projects]
>
> Act as an expert who uses [the skill] for paid work every day and has
> trained a lot of people to do the same.
>
> Build me a learning plan with:
>
> 1. The few parts of this skill that give most of the results
> 2. A week-by-week plan for [time], with what to learn and one real
>    project to build each week
> 3. The best resources for each week (name real courses, channels or
>    docs, and tell me if you're not sure something exists)
> 4. 3 portfolio projects that prove I have this skill, from easy to hard
> 5. The mistakes people at my level usually make, and how to avoid them
> 6. How I'll know I'm ready: what I should be able to do by the end
> 7. How people make money with this skill: who pays for it, what they
>    pay for, and how I can find my first paying client or job
>
> RULES
> - skip the basics I already know, start from where I am
> - be honest about what's hard and how long things really take
> - everything has to fit my time and budget
>
> Before you start, ask me up to 5 questions about anything that's unclear.
> After that, I'll tell you every week what I did, and you'll adjust the plan.
> ```

Check any course or channel it names before you pay for it. The prompt asks it to flag doubts, but it can still name things that don't exist.

> [!DO] Start your learning plan
> Fill in the brackets of the learning-plan prompt for one skill, paste it into your AI assistant, answer its questions, and save the chat with a name you'll find next week.

> [!CHECK] In a 30-day plan for learning an AI skill, what happens each week?
> Week 1, pick one skill and one real problem; week 2, build version 1; week 3, use it on real work and log the time saved; week 4, share how you built it and offer it to 3 people.

> [!CHECK] You've listed every task in your week. Which ones get an AI assistant?
> The ones where AI drafts and you fix, out of three groups: AI does it alone, AI drafts and you fix, AI can't help yet. Time each one before and after.

> [!CHECK] When AI drafts most of a task, why is the last 20% the part people pay for?
> Choosing, fixing and catching mistakes is the skill. AI writes 50 ads in a minute; knowing which 3 to test is the job.
