> [!TLDR]
> - Busy founders and creators pay one AI-equipped person to run their content as a creative director.
> - An agent that already saves you hours every week can be sold to businesses with the same task.
> - Sell outbound only to companies whose offer already sells: high price, proof in a case study.
> - When AI cuts your delivery time, keep the price and take the margin.

## Be the creative team a busy founder lacks

Founders and creators who have no time for content will pay one person who uses AI well to act as their creative director, for a fraction of what a team costs. Dan Koe describes two versions: be your own creative team (personal brand, audience, digital products, email list), or be one for clients.

The gap Koe sees in clients: most founders still post "announcement" content and don't know how influence is built online. Agencies Koe spoke to say AI moved the human job to **reviewing and rewriting drafts**. One said it now takes 4 hours a day instead of 12.

Koe puts the range at $10k to $75k a month across 1 to 3 clients, "if you're good at this and practice", and warns it is not a quick win. Koe also sells Eden, a tool for exactly this work, so weigh the numbers accordingly. The skills come from *Write down what makes you you* and *Plan six months of ideas in one sitting*.

## Resell an agent you already run

An agent that replaces a recurring manual task, like a weekly competitor report, saves you hours. Other businesses with the same task will pay for it. Build it for yourself first (see *Run marketing as code*), then sell it.

- Route the results to yourself for approval first.
- Run it for 2 to 3 weeks, fix what breaks, and count the hours saved.
- That count is your pitch. The log from your week audit in *Learn a new skill in 30 days* works the same way.

The author, @notkvtwts, gives this as an illustration with no results attached.

## Sell outbound only where the offer already sells

A done-for-you LinkedIn outreach service works best for B2B companies with high-priced offers that already sell. One new customer for them covers months of your fee, and a case study with a number proves the offer already sells.

> [!TRICK] Find and pitch outbound clients
> 1. Go to linkedin.com/jobs and search "sdr". Every company that shows up already pays a full salary to get sales calls booked.
> 2. Keep only companies that charge customers $10k or more and whose website has a case study with a number in it.
> 3. Pitch the founder a test: 1,000 DMs to their buyers for $500. The replies from the test are what sell the monthly retainer.
> 4. The author's math: 1 call per 100 DMs, so 10 calls per 1,000. At $2,500 a month per client, 4 clients is $10k and 8 is $20k.
>
> The author, @DinScales26, sells this service and a sending tool, and the 1% call rate is assumed, not shown. Treat it as a number to test. For writing and testing the DMs themselves, see *Open DMs with a problem buyers already pay to solve*.

## Keep your price when AI cuts the hours

Clients pay for the result, not your time. When AI shrinks the work, keep the price and **take the margin**.

Kyle Hunt's example: an agency audit sold at $5,000 used to take 5 hours by hand. After feeding AI every past audit transcript plus the frameworks used, it takes the AI about 30 minutes, then 30 minutes of human checking. The price stayed at $5,000. That's one self-reported example from someone who sells agency consulting.

For the checking half, see *Use AI without surrendering your judgment*. See *Read your pricing from your numbers* for when to raise prices.

> [!DO] Write a three-line offer
> Pick one task you already do well with AI. In a note, write three lines: who pays for it, the result they get, and the price you'd charge for that result.

> [!CHECK] When you sell outbound outreach as a service, which companies make good clients?
> B2B companies with high-priced offers that already sell, shown by a case study with a number in it.

> [!CHECK] AI cuts the time to deliver your $5,000 audit from 5 hours to 1. What happens to the price?
> It stays at $5,000. Clients pay for the result, not your time, so you take the margin.

> [!CHECK] Why run an agent for yourself for a few weeks before you sell it to other businesses?
> Running it fixes what breaks and gives you a count of hours saved, and that count is your pitch.
